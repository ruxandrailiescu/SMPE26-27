# The publication system

- generally neither the author/s or reviewer/s get monetary rewards from
  the process; all proceedings go to the publishing house; back in the
  day, it was justified for the printing and typing efforts.
- scientists can pay the publishing house in order to make their work
  available to the public in an open access manner (aka no fees).
- HAL, arXiv are places where researchers can make their work available
  to the general public; not peer-reviewed.
- differences between fields: for example, in computer science,
  conference proceedings are comparable to journal publications in other
  fields (e.g., NeurIPS, ICML, etc.).
- the reviewing process should help the researcher/s refine their work
  so that it can be publishable; there exist different thresholds: no
  revisions, minor revisions, major revisions, refusal.
- metrics to evaluate researchers: impact factor, h-index (and many
  variations).

# University ranking system
